\section{Introduction}

\section{GG-KM}

The GG-KM (an acronym for Generalized Gamma Distribution and Kernel
Machines) is a novel semiparametric approach to long-term survival
modeling. The main objective is to address the limitations of linear
predictors by employing kernel functions to model
$\log\left(\theta(\mathbf{x})\right)$ within a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert
Space (RKHS), where $\theta(\mathbf{x})$ is a parameter governing the
distribution of the latent number of competing causes in the promotion
cure model. This formulation allows for a flexible, potentially
nonlinear relationship between the covariates and the latent competing-risk
structure.

To provide greater flexibility in modeling the survival distribution of
susceptible individuals, the Generalized Gamma distribution, introduced by \citeonline{stacy1962generalization}
is adopted for the latent event times. This distribution includes several commonly used
parametric survival distributions as special cases and, consequently,
allows a broad range of hazard shapes to be represented. Its probability density
and cumulative density functions are defined as:
\begin{equation}
f(t; a,d,p)
=
\frac{p t^{d-1}\exp\left[-(t/a)^p\right]}
{a^d\Gamma(d/p)},
\qquad
F(t; a,d,p)
=
\frac{\gamma\left(d/p,(t/a)^p\right)}
{\Gamma(d/p)}
\tag{3}
\end{equation}
where $a> 0$ is the scale parameter, and $d$, $p> 0$ are the shape parameters. The
Generalized Gamma distribution is a powerful generalization. For example, if $d=p$, it reduces
to the Weibull distribution. Also, if $p=1$ or $d/p \to \infty$ it reduces to the
Standard Gamma and converges to the Log-Normal distribution, respectively.


The model is estimated through an Expectation--Maximization (EM) \cite{mclachlan2008algorithm}
algorithm and is formulated for different probability distributions of the
latent number of competing causes $N$, namely the Bernoulli, Binomial,
Poisson, and Negative Binomial distributions. This formulation provides a
unified framework for investigating different assumptions regarding the
latent competing-risk structure while preserving the kernel-based
semiparametric formulation.

\subsection{Nonparametric extension via kernel methods}

Although the classical promotion-time cure model allows covariates to be
incorporated through parametric functions of $\theta(\mathbf{x})$, such an
approach may be restrictive when the relationship between the covariates
and the latent competing-risk structure is complex or nonlinear.

In this context, the proposition is to model the parameter governing the
distribution of the latent competing causes, $\theta(\mathbf{x})$, through
a nonparametric function
\begin{equation}
g(\mathbf{x}) = \log\left(\theta(\mathbf{x})\right),
\end{equation}
where $g:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}$. The logarithmic
transformation ensures that $\theta(\mathbf{x})>0$ for every
$\mathbf{x}$. The estimation of $g$ is formulated through the minimization
of a penalized negative log-likelihood:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ggkm_penalized_likelihood}
\min_{g \in \mathcal{H}_K}
\left\{
-\sum_{i=1}^{n} l_i(g)
+
\frac{\lambda}{2}\|g\|_{\mathcal{H}_K}^{2}
\right\},
\end{equation}
where $l_i(g)$ denotes the log-likelihood contribution of the $i$th
individual, $\mathcal{H}_K$ is a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS)
associated with a positive-definite kernel $K(\cdot,\cdot)$, and
$\lambda\geq 0$ is a regularization parameter controlling the complexity
of the estimated function.

By the Representer Theorem, the solution to the optimization problem in
Equation~\eqref{eq:ggkm_penalized_likelihood} admits a finite-dimensional
representation of the form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ggkm_representer}
g^*(\mathbf{x})
=
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_i K(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{x}_i),
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\in\mathbb{R}$ are coefficients to be
estimated. Consequently, the parameter governing the distribution of the
latent competing causes for an individual with covariate vector
$\mathbf{x}_i$ is given by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ggkm_theta}
\theta(\mathbf{x}_i)
=
\exp\left\{
\sum_{j=1}^{n}
\alpha_j K(\mathbf{x}_i,\mathbf{x}_j)
\right\}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Estimation Procedures}

The estimation of the proposed models is performed using the
Expectation--Maximization (EM) algorithm. Since the number of latent
competing causes $N_i$ is unobserved, direct maximization of the
observed-data likelihood may be analytically and computationally
challenging. The EM algorithm addresses this difficulty by treating
$N_i$, $i=1,\ldots,n$, as latent variables and iteratively maximizing
the expected complete-data log-likelihood.

Let $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{obs}} = \left\{ (t_i,\delta_i,\mathbf{x}_i) \right\}^{n}_{i=1}$
denote the observed data and let $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{comp}} = \left\{ (t_i,\delta_i,n_i,\mathbf{x}_i) \right\}^{n}_{i=1}$
denote the corresponding complete data. Let
$\boldsymbol{\eta}$ denote the vector of model parameters. The
observed-data likelihood is obtained by integrating out the latent
variables:
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{obs}}
\left(
\boldsymbol{\eta};
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{obs}}
\right)
=
\sum_{\boldsymbol{n}}
L_c
\left(
\boldsymbol{\eta};
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{comp}}
\right),
\end{equation}
where the summation is taken over all possible realizations of
$\boldsymbol{n}=(n_1,\ldots,n_n)$ according to the support of the
corresponding distribution of $N_i$.

The complete-data log-likelihood can be shown to be Table~\ref{tab:likelihood_expectation_distributions}
and their proof are described in Appendix~\ref{app:complete_data_likelihood}. These
results are used as teh basis for the EM procedure. Given a current
parameter estimate $\boldsymbol{\eta}^{(r)}$, the E-step consists of
computing the conditional expectation
\begin{equation}
Q\left(
\boldsymbol{\eta}\mid\boldsymbol{\eta}^{(r)}
\right)
=
\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\eta}^{(r)}}
\left[
\ell_c
\left(
\boldsymbol{\eta};
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{comp}}
\right)
\mid
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{obs}}
\right].
\label{eq:generic_Q_function}
\end{equation}

In particular, the expectation is taken with respect to the conditional
distribution of the latent number of competing causes,
\[
P_{\boldsymbol{\eta}^{(r)}}
\left(
N_i=n_i
\mid
t_i,\delta_i,\mathbf{x}_i
\right).
\]
Consequently, the quantities involving $N_i$ in the complete-data
log-likelihood are replaced by their corresponding conditional
expectations under the current parameter estimates.

In the M-step, the expected complete-data log-likelihood is maximized
with respect to the model parameters
$\boldsymbol{\eta}^{(r+1)}
=
\underset{\boldsymbol{\eta}}{\arg\max}
\;
Q\left(
\boldsymbol{\eta}\mid\boldsymbol{\eta}^{(r)}
\right)$.

Because the proposed GG-KM model estimates the nonparametric function
$g(\mathbf{x})$ in an RKHS, regularization is incorporated into the
M-step. Thus, the corresponding Penalized EM algorithm maximizes
$Q_{\lambda}\left(\boldsymbol{\eta}\mid\boldsymbol{\eta}^{(r)}\right) = Q\left( \boldsymbol{\eta}\mid\boldsymbol{\eta}^{(r)} \right) - \frac{\lambda}{2} \left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_K}^{2}$.
The resulting update is therefore $\boldsymbol{\eta}^{(r+1)} = \underset{\boldsymbol{\eta}}{\arg\max} \; Q_{\lambda} \left( \boldsymbol{\eta}\mid\boldsymbol{\eta}^{(r)}\right)$.

For the Bernoulli, Binomial, and Poisson specifications, the resulting
optimization problems can be handled directly within this penalized EM
framework. However, the Negative Binomial formulation introduces the
additional dispersion parameter $\alpha$. In this case, the expected
complete-data log-likelihood contains terms involving the Gamma
function and $\alpha$, which lead to a considerably more complicated
optimization problem. In particular, the expectation required for the
update of $\alpha$ does not generally admit a convenient closed-form
expression and may be computationally expensive to evaluate repeatedly
during the M-step.

To address this difficulty, the ECME (Expectation/Conditional
Maximization Either) algorithm is considered for the Negative Binomial
model \cite{mclachlan2008algorithm}. ECME is an extension of the EM
framework in which some parameter blocks are updated by maximizing the
expected complete-data log-likelihood, as in the conventional EM
algorithm, while other parameter blocks may instead be updated by
directly maximizing the observed-data log-likelihood.

Thus, for the Negative Binomial model, the ECME procedure is used
specifically for the estimation of the dispersion parameter $\alpha$.
Rather than requiring the maximization of a complicated expected
complete-data contribution with respect to $\alpha$, the observed-data
log-likelihood can be used for this parameter block. The remaining
parameters are updated through the usual penalized conditional
maximization steps. This strategy preserves the EM framework while
avoiding an unnecessarily complicated computation in the estimation of
$\alpha$.

\subsection{GG-KM with Poisson}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Penalized EM algorithm for the GG-KM model -
$N_i \sim \mathrm{Poisson}\!\bigl(\theta(\mathbf{x}_i)\bigr)$.}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]

\State Initialize $\alpha^{(0)},a^{(0)},d^{(0)},p^{(0)}$, set $k\gets0$, and choose $\varepsilon>0$.

\While{$|\ell_P^{(k)}-\ell_P^{(k-1)}|>\varepsilon$}

\State \textbf{E-step:} for $i=1,\ldots,n$, compute
\[
\widehat N_i^{(k+1)}
=
\delta_i+
\exp\!\bigl(\mathbf{K}_i^\top\alpha^{(k)}\bigr)
S\!\left(t_i;a^{(k)},d^{(k)},p^{(k)}\right),
\]
where $\mathbf{K}_i=(K(x_i,x_1),\ldots,K(x_i,x_n))^\top$.

\State \textbf{M-step:} update
\[
\alpha^{(k+1)}
=
\arg\max_{\alpha}
\left\{
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\left[
-\exp(\mathbf{K}_i^\top\alpha)
+\widehat N_i^{(k+1)}\mathbf{K}_i^\top\alpha
\right]
-\frac{\lambda}{2}\alpha^\top K\alpha
\right\},
\]
and
\[
(a^{(k+1)},d^{(k+1)},p^{(k+1)})
=
\arg\max_{a,d,p}
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\left[
\delta_i\log f(t_i;a,d,p)
+
(\widehat N_i^{(k+1)}-\delta_i)
\log S(t_i;a,d,p)
\right].
\]

\State Compute
\[
\ell_P^{(k+1)}
=
\ell_P\!\left(
\alpha^{(k+1)},a^{(k+1)},d^{(k+1)},p^{(k+1)}
\right),
\qquad k\gets k+1.
\]

\EndWhile

\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


\subsection{GG-KM with Negative Binomial}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{ECME algorithm for estimating the GG-KM model with
Negative Binomial latent risks.}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]

\State Initialize
$\alpha^{(0)},a^{(0)},d^{(0)},p^{(0)}$, set $k\gets0$, and choose
$\varepsilon>0$.

\Repeat

\State For $i=1,\ldots,n$, compute
\[
\theta_i^{(k)}
=
\exp\!\left(
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_j^{(k)}K(x_i,x_j)
\right),
\qquad
S^{(k)}(t_i)
=
1-F\!\left(t_i;a^{(k)},d^{(k)},p^{(k)}\right).
\]

\State \textbf{E-step:} compute
\[
\widehat N_i^{(k+1)}
=
\delta_i+
\frac{
\bigl(1+\alpha^{(k)}\delta_i\bigr)
\theta_i^{(k)}S^{(k)}(t_i)
}{
1+\alpha^{(k)}\theta_i^{(k)}
\left[1-S^{(k)}(t_i)\right]
},
\qquad i=1,\ldots,n.
\]

\State \textbf{M-step:} update
\[
\alpha^{(k+1)}
=
\arg\max_{\alpha}
\left\{
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\left[
\widehat N_i^{(k+1)}\log\theta_i
-
\left(
\widehat N_i^{(k+1)}+\frac{1}{\alpha^{(k)}}
\right)
\log\!\bigl(1+\alpha^{(k)}\theta_i\bigr)
\right]
-\frac{\lambda}{2}\alpha^\top K\alpha
\right\},
\]
\[
(a^{(k+1)},d^{(k+1)},p^{(k+1)})
=
\arg\max_{a,d,p}
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\left[
\delta_i\log f(t_i;a,d,p)
+
(\widehat N_i^{(k+1)}-\delta_i)
\log S(t_i;a,d,p)
\right].
\]

\State \textbf{CM-step:} update
\[
\alpha^{(k+1)}
=
\arg\max_{\alpha>0}
\ell_O\!\left(
\alpha^{(k+1)},a^{(k+1)},d^{(k+1)},p^{(k+1)},\alpha
\right).
\]

\State Compute
\[
\ell_P^{(k+1)}
=
\ell_P\!\left(
\alpha^{(k+1)},a^{(k+1)},d^{(k+1)},p^{(k+1)},\alpha^{(k+1)}
\right),
\qquad
\Delta^{(k+1)}
=
\left|\ell_P^{(k+1)}-\ell_P^{(k)}\right|,
\]
and set $k\gets k+1$.

\Until{$\Delta^{(k+1)}<\varepsilon$}

\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{GG-KM with Binomial}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Penalized EM algorithm for the GG-KM model -
$N_i \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(K,\theta_i^*)$.}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]

\State Initialize
$\alpha^{(0)},a^{(0)},d^{(0)},p^{(0)}$, set $k\gets0$, and choose
$\varepsilon>0$.

\Repeat

\State \textbf{E-step:} for $i=1,\ldots,n$, compute
\[
g_i^{(k)}
=
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_j^{(k)}K(x_i,x_j),
\qquad
\theta_i^{*(k)}
=
\frac{\exp(g_i^{(k)})}{1+\exp(g_i^{(k)})},
\]
\[
\widehat N_i^{(k+1)}
=
\delta_i+
(K-\delta_i)
\frac{
\theta_i^{*(k)}
S(t_i;a^{(k)},d^{(k)},p^{(k)})
}{
1-\theta_i^{*(k)}
+\theta_i^{*(k)}
S(t_i;a^{(k)},d^{(k)},p^{(k)})
}.
\]

\State \textbf{M-step:} update
\[
\alpha^{(k+1)}
=
\arg\max_{\alpha}
\left\{
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\left[
\widehat N_i^{(k+1)}g_i
-
K\log\!\bigl(1+\exp(g_i)\bigr)
\right]
-\frac{\lambda}{2}\alpha^\top K\alpha
\right\}\text{,}
\]
\[
(a^{(k+1)},d^{(k+1)},p^{(k+1)})
=
\arg\max_{a,d,p}
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\left[
\delta_i\log f(t_i;a,d,p)
+
(\widehat N_i^{(k+1)}-\delta_i)
\log S(t_i;a,d,p)
\right].
\]

\State Compute
\[
\ell_P^{(k+1)}
=
\ell_P\!\left(
\alpha^{(k+1)},a^{(k+1)},d^{(k+1)},p^{(k+1)}
\right),
\qquad
\Delta^{(k+1)}
=
\left|\ell_P^{(k+1)}-\ell_P^{(k)}\right|,
\]
and set $k\gets k+1$.

\Until{$\Delta^{(k+1)}<\varepsilon$}

\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Extension via Gradient Boosting}

As an alternative to the kernel-based representation of the nonlinear predictor,
it is also considered a gradient boosting approach. Gradient boosting can be
interpreted as an optimization procedure in function space, in which an unknown
function is estimated through a stagewise additive expansion of weak learners
\cite{friedman2001greedy,mason1999boosting}. In this context, the
function governing the latent competing-risk distribution is represented as
\begin{equation}
g(\mathbf{x})
=
g_0(\mathbf{x})
+
\sum_{m=1}^{M}
\nu \rho_m h_m(\mathbf{x}),
\end{equation}
where $h_m(\mathbf{x})$ denotes a regression tree, $\rho_m$ is obtained numerically
using the L-BFGS-B algorithm, and $\nu\in(0,1]$ is a shrinkage parameter.

The boosting procedure is incorporated into the EM algorithm by replacing the
kernel-based maximization step for $g(\mathbf{x})$ with a functional gradient
optimization. At each EM iteration, after computing the conditional expectations
$\widehat{N}_i=E(N_i\mid t_i,\delta_i,\mathbf{x}_i)$, the functional gradient of
the expected complete-data log-likelihood with respect to $g$ is evaluated at the
current fitted values. A regression tree is then fitted to these gradient values
to approximate the direction of increase of the expected complete-data
log-likelihood, and the predictor is updated in the direction defined by the
fitted tree.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{EM algorithm with gradient boosting -
\label{alg:functional_gradient_boosting}
$N_i \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(K,\theta_i^*)$.}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]

\State Initialize
$g^{(0)}(\cdot),a^{(0)},d^{(0)},p^{(0)}$,
set $k\gets0$, and choose $\varepsilon>0$, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{tree}}>0$,
learning rate $\nu\in(0,1]$, and maximum number of trees $M$.

\While{$|\ell^{(k)}-\ell^{(k-1)}|>\varepsilon$}

\State \textbf{E-step:} for $i=1,\ldots,n$, compute
\[
\theta_i^{*(k)}
=
\frac{\exp\{g^{(k)}(\mathbf{x}_i)\}}
     {1+\exp\{g^{(k)}(\mathbf{x}_i)\}},
\]
and
\[
\widehat N_i^{(k+1)}
=
\delta_i
+
(K-\delta_i)
\frac{
\theta_i^{*(k)}
S(t_i;a^{(k)},d^{(k)},p^{(k)})
}{
1-\theta_i^{*(k)}
+
\theta_i^{*(k)}
S(t_i;a^{(k)},d^{(k)},p^{(k)})
}.
\]

\State \textbf{M-step for $g$:} set $g_0\gets g^{(k)}$ and, for
$m=1,\ldots,M$, compute the functional gradient
\[
\mathcal{G}_{i,m}
=
\widehat N_i^{(k+1)}
-
K
\frac{\exp\{g_{m-1}(\mathbf{x}_i)\}}
     {1+\exp\{g_{m-1}(\mathbf{x}_i)\}},
\qquad i=1,\ldots,n,
\]
fit a regression tree $h_m$ to
$\{(\mathbf{x}_i,\mathcal{G}_{i,m})\}_{i=1}^n$, and obtain
\[
\rho_m
=
\arg\max_{\rho\geq0}
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\left[
\widehat N_i^{(k+1)}
\{g_{m-1}(\mathbf{x}_i)+\rho h_m(\mathbf{x}_i)\}
-
K\log
\left(
1+\exp\{g_{m-1}(\mathbf{x}_i)+\rho h_m(\mathbf{x}_i)\}
\right)
\right].
\]

\State Update
\[
g_m(\mathbf{x})
=
g_{m-1}(\mathbf{x})
+
\nu\rho_m h_m(\mathbf{x}),
\]
stopping the boosting iterations if
\[
\frac{1}{n}
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\left|
g_m(\mathbf{x}_i)-g_{m-1}(\mathbf{x}_i)
\right|
<
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{tree}}.
\]
Set $g^{(k+1)}\gets g_m$.

\State Update
\[
(a^{(k+1)},d^{(k+1)},p^{(k+1)})
=
\arg\max_{a,d,p}
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\left[
\delta_i\log f(t_i;a,d,p)
+
(\widehat N_i^{(k+1)}-\delta_i)
\log S(t_i;a,d,p)
\right].
\]

\State Compute
\[
\ell^{(k+1)}
=
\ell\!\left(
g^{(k+1)},a^{(k+1)},d^{(k+1)},p^{(k+1)}
\right),
\qquad
k\gets k+1.
\]

\EndWhile

\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The specific functional gradient depends on the assumed distribution of the latent
number of competing causes. Therefore, the same general boosting framework can be
applied to the Poisson, Binomial, and Negative Binomial formulations
by deriving the corresponding gradient from each expected complete-data
log-likelihood. The remaining parameters of the Generalized Gamma distribution are
updated through the same EM conditional maximization step used in the kernel-based
models. Algorithm~\ref{alg:functional_gradient_boosting} represents the specific
case where the latent variable $N$ follows a Binomial distribution.
